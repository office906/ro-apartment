Living in Prenzlauer Berg: a newcomer's guide

Prenzlauer Berg is a residential district in north-east central Berlin, part of the borough of Pankow. It is known for its late-19th and early-20th-century buildings, tree-lined streets, squares with cafés and playgrounds, and a quiet, settled pace compared to Mitte or Neukölln.

Around Helmholtzplatz

Helmholtzplatz is one of the area's central squares, surrounded by cafés, restaurants, small shops and bakeries. Within walking distance:

  • Kollwitzplatz, with its weekly farmers' market.
  • Mauerpark, a large park along the former Berlin Wall strip, busy on Sundays with its flea market.
  • Kulturbrauerei, a former brewery complex with a cinema, venues and the Museum in der Kulturbrauerei on everyday life in the GDR.
  • Schönhauser Allee, the main shopping street, with supermarkets, drugstores and the Schönhauser Allee Arcaden.

Getting around

From the apartment, S-Bahn Prenzlauer Allee is a 5-minute walk. It is on the Ringbahn (S41/S42), the circle line that connects to most other S-Bahn and U-Bahn lines. Tram M2 (2 minutes) runs to Alexanderplatz; tram M10 (5 minutes) runs east–west across Prenzlauer Berg and Friedrichshain. Berlin is flat and cycling is common.

Your first weeks in Berlin

  • Anmeldung. Everyone who moves into a flat in Germany must register their address within two weeks. You need your passport and the landlord confirmation (Wohnungsgeberbestätigung), which you receive with this apartment. Appointments are booked at service.berlin.de.
  • Tax ID. Your tax identification number (Steuer-ID) is sent by post to your registered address a few weeks after Anmeldung. Employers need it for payroll.
  • Bank account and health insurance usually need a registered address, which is why a flat with Anmeldung matters in the first months.
  • Looking for a permanent flat takes time in Berlin. A furnished base for 2 to 6 months lets you search calmly and view flats in person.
